AARP reports the FBI's estimate that skimming costs more than $1 billion a year. Skimmers are built to look like part of the machine, so the checks below are about touch and comparison, not just looking.

## Spot 1: gas pumps

- Wiggle the card reader. If it moves, use another pump or pay inside.
- Compare it with the reader on the next pump.
- Many stations put a security seal on the pump panel. A broken seal, or one that reads "VOID", means the panel has been opened.
- Pumps close to the store are watched more than those at the far end.

## Spot 2: ATMs

- Pull gently on the card slot and look for anything stuck on.
- Cover the keypad with your other hand while you type your PIN.
- An ATM inside a bank branch is harder to tamper with.
- If your card gets stuck, call your bank from where you are.

## Spot 3: self-checkouts

- Card terminals can have an overlay that sits on top of the real one. The U.S. Secret Service suggests pulling on the corners.

## Tap instead

Tapping your card, or paying with your phone's wallet, means a skimmer can't read your card's magnetic stripe.

## If it already happened

- Call your card company or bank straight away.
- In the US, the FTC says your liability for a credit card is capped at $50. For a debit card, it depends on how fast you report it.
- Go to [I've been scammed](/been-scammed/) for where to report.
