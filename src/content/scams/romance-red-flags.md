The FTC's number one rule: never send money or gifts to a sweetheart you haven't met in person. Romance scammers build trust for weeks or months, then invent a reason they need money: medical bills, a plane ticket to visit you, fees to get out of trouble, or help to "invest" in crypto. They ask for payment methods that are hard to reverse, like wire transfers, gift cards, payment apps or cryptocurrency.

In the first nine months of 2025, people reported losing more than $1.16 billion to romance scams to the FTC, according to USA TODAY's report on the FTC's data.

## What to do

- Stop writing to the person.
- Talk to a friend or family member you trust about your new match. If they're worried, listen.
- Do a reverse image search of their profile photo. If it shows up under other names, it's a scam.
- Search their job with the word "scammer" to see if others have heard the same story.
- Report the profile to the dating app or site.

## Already sent money?

- Contact your bank, the wire service, the gift card company or the crypto provider right away. Tell them you paid a scammer and ask for a refund.
- Report it: in the US at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, in the UK to Report Fraud, in Canada to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, in Australia to Scamwatch.
- Watch out for a second scam: anyone who offers to get your money back for a fee is another scammer.
