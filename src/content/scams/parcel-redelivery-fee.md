Fake package delivery texts are one of the most reported text scams in the US, according to the FTC. People who tapped the link reported paying a small "redelivery fee" that turned out to be a trick to get their card number, and sometimes more personal details. The same texts go out under the names of postal services and couriers in the UK, Canada and Australia.

The BBB says USPS won't text or email you about a delivery unless you signed up for alerts for that package.

## What to do

- Don't tap the link or reply.
- Check your parcel yourself in the courier's or store's own app, or on a website you type in.
- Forward the text to 7726 (US, UK and Canada), then delete it. In Australia, report it at scamwatch.gov.au.

## Already entered your card details?

- Call your bank or card company now, using the number on the back of your card.
