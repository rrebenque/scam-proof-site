The story changes every time: an unpaid fine, a tax bill, a hacked bank account, a grandchild in trouble. The way they ask to be paid doesn't change. The US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) says anyone who insists you pay by gift card, crypto, a payment app or a wire transfer is a scammer.

## The five

1. **Gift cards.** Once you read out the numbers on the back, the money is gone. Gift cards are for gifts.
2. **Wire transfers.** Wiring money is like sending cash.
3. **Bitcoin and crypto ATMs.** No real government office or business sends you to a crypto machine. The FBI has warned about rising complaints involving these kiosks.
4. **Payment apps to a stranger.** Apps like Zelle work like cash. Use them only with people you know. [Read our Zelle guide](/scams/zelle/).
5. **Cash or gold to a courier.** Real government agents don't ask you to buy gold or hand over cash to someone at your door.

## What to do instead

- Hang up, and look up the real office's number yourself.
- For shopping, pay by credit card when you can.
- See [which payments protect you](/how-to-pay-safely/) for a full comparison.
