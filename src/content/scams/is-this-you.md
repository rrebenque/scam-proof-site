The message comes from someone you trust, which is exactly why it works. Your friend didn't send it: their account was taken over the same way. The US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) says people reported losing $2.1 billion in 2025 to scams that started on social media, more than any other way scammers made contact.

## What to do

- Don't tap the link, and don't reply in the same chat. The scammer is reading it.
- Contact your friend another way, such as a phone call or text, so they can secure their account.
- Turn on two-factor login for your Facebook account, so a stolen password alone isn't enough.

## Already typed your password?

- Change your Facebook password straight away, and turn on two-factor login.
- Log out of any devices you don't recognise. If you're locked out, go to facebook.com/hacked.
- If you used the same password anywhere else, change it there too, starting with your email.
- Tell your friends not to open links or send money from your account.
- Go to [I've been scammed](/been-scammed/) if money was taken.
