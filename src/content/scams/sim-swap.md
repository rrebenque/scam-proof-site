Your phone number is the key to many accounts, because so many sign-in codes are sent by text. In a SIM swap, a scammer convinces your phone company to move that number to a SIM card they control. U.S. PIRG reports 982 complaints to the FBI in 2024, with about $26 million lost.

## Lock your number today

- Call your phone company and add a PIN or password to your account. Ask if they offer a lock on SIM changes or number transfers.
- Where you can, use an authentication app for sign-in codes instead of text messages.
- Don't post your phone number, birthday or address publicly. Scammers use them to sound like you.

## If your phone suddenly says "No service"

- Contact your phone company from another phone, or in a store, straight away.
- Call your bank and ask them to watch for unusual activity.
- Change your email password first, then your banking passwords.
- Go to [I've been scammed](/been-scammed/) to report it.
