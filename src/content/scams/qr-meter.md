Cities in the US and Canada have warned about fake QR codes on parking meters and pay stations. In Kelowna, Canada, police investigated after fraudulent codes were found on 75 meters. The trick is simple: a sticker over or next to the real payment option sends you to a look-alike payment page.

## Why it works

Paying by QR code is normal now, and you're usually in a hurry when you park. A sticker on an official-looking meter borrows the city's trust.

## What to do

- Before you scan, look closely. Does the code look like a sticker? Is it covering something?
- Pay at the meter itself, or in your city's official parking app that you downloaded from your phone's app store.
- If a page opens after scanning, check the web address before you type anything.
- Report the sticker to your city or parking authority, so they can remove it.
- If you already paid through a fake page, call your card company now. Go to [I've been scammed](/been-scammed/) for the next steps.
