The right move is the same in every country: hang up, then call back on a number you already have saved. A family safe word makes this even stronger. See [Family Safe Word: How To Set One Up](/scams/safe-word/).

## What the FTC and FBI say

- The US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) says a scammer needs only a short recording of a relative's voice, which could come from something posted online. The FTC doesn't give a number of seconds.
- In one test, McAfee's researchers produced an 85% voice match from just three seconds of audio. That is a security company's finding, not an official figure.
- Scammers push for payments that are hard to reverse: wire transfers, cryptocurrency, payment apps and gift cards.
- The FBI advises agreeing a secret word or phrase with family, hanging up and calling back on a number you already know, and keeping social media accounts private.
- Report scams at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov).
