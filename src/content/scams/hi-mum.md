This scam is known as "Hi Mum" or "Hi Dad". It's often sent on WhatsApp, but also by text. SBS News reports more than 11,000 incidents in Australia in 2022, with losses over $7.2 million. Newer versions use a cloned voice on a phone call.

## The one move that beats it

Call your child on the number you already have saved. If it really is them, they'll answer or call back. The US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) gives the same advice: call your loved one on a number you know is right.

## Other ways to protect yourself

- Agree on a [family safe word](/scams/safe-word/) that a scammer can't know.
- Never send money to an account that isn't in your child's name because of a text.
- Block and report the number in your messaging app.

## Already paid?

- Call your bank straight away and ask them to stop or recall the payment.
- Watch out for a second scam: Scamwatch says one in three scam victims is scammed more than once, often by someone offering to recover the money.
- See [I've been scammed](/been-scammed/) for where to report.
