Zelle is like cash. Only send it to people you know.

## Why it is hard to get back

The FTC says a payment app linked to your bank account or debit card is almost like handing someone cash, and once you send money it is nearly impossible to get it back. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) notes that you can sometimes dispute a credit card charge, but mobile payment services generally don't have a recall or retrieval feature. That is why paying a stranger by credit card is safer.

## If a scammer got into your account

The CFPB's guidance says that if a scammer tricks you into sharing your login details, a texted confirmation code or card information, and then uses them to start the transfer, that transfer is treated as unauthorized. Report it to your bank right away and say exactly how it happened. The CFPB's guidance does not address a payment you send yourself, such as paying a fake ticket seller.

This page applies to the US. Refund rules for instant payments differ in the UK, Canada and Australia.
