Relationship investment scams, often called pig butchering, start with contact that looks like a mistake, such as a "wrong number" text. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) describes the pattern: the scammer builds trust over weeks or months, then points you to a fake trading website or app, often using cryptocurrency. Fake screens show growing balances, and a small withdrawal may be allowed to build confidence. When you try to cash out, you are told to pay fees or taxes first.

## How big is it?

The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) reports that cryptocurrency investment fraud cost Americans about $7.2 billion in 2025, across 61,559 complaints. Complaints rose 48% and losses rose 25% from 2024. The FBI says these scams usually start by text message, on social media, through ads or on dating apps. The FTC also lists "wrong number" texts, which often turn into romantic chats and then investment fraud.

## What to do

- Don't reply to unexpected texts from unknown senders. Block and report them.
- Don't invest on the advice of someone who contacted you first.
- Never pay fees or taxes to release money from an investment.
- Be wary of anyone who asks you to pay in cryptocurrency or at a bitcoin ATM.
- Forward the text to 7726, and in the US report it at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov) and the FBI's [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov).
